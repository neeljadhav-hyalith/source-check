# Research brief

Paste everything below the line into any deep research tool. Fill the 4 bracketed sections. Leave the rules unchanged: they are what make the report checkable.

---

RESEARCH TASK

[1. RESEARCH QUESTION]
{1 paragraph. 1 specific question a decision depends on.}

[2. WHO IS ASKING AND WHY]
{Who you are, where you operate, team size, and the decision this will feed. Example: "I run a 6-person software company in India. This decides which analytics tool we buy for 12 users."}

[3. SCOPE]
{Companies, products, regions and time window in scope. Name the exact page URLs that must be read: naming a domain does not cause it to be visited. Name what is out of scope and any sources to avoid.}

[4. DELIVERABLES]
{What you want back, and in what shape: a table, a ranked list, a short memo.}

SOURCE RULES
Use: the original documents. Official product documentation and terms; company filings and annual reports; the company's own press releases about itself; regulator and court documents; official statistics with a published method; peer-reviewed papers; bylined reporting in established publications; funding databases for funding data only; review sites only as user opinion.
Do not use: blogs outside established publications, listicles, SEO content, social posts, forums, AI-written pages, vendor marketing about rivals, "industry research" with no published method, or encyclopaedias for primary facts.
A page that quotes a primary source is not the primary source. Open the original, or mark the claim UNVERIFIABLE.

TAG EVERY CLAIM
[PRIMARY] verified in a primary source. Give the name, URL and date.
[SECONDARY] supported by a secondary source. Give the name, URL and date.
[INFERRED] derived by you. Show the step.
[UNVERIFIABLE] not verified. Do not guess a value.
A claim below SECONDARY is not asserted.

HOW-IT-WORKS CLAIMS
For any claim about how a product or method works, quote the exact sentence from the vendor's own documentation.

SAME PRODUCT, SAME SURFACE
A policy, metric or feature belongs to the exact product, tier, region and surface the source names. Do not carry it to a sibling product, another region, or from ads to organic, or the reverse. If you think the sibling has its own version, find it.

ABSENCE
Write "not found in the sources searched" and list the queries run and pages opened. Never state a flat absence.

PRICES
For each product: the pricing unit (per user, flat, usage), whether free viewer or guest access exists (quote the vendor), the cheapest tier with the needed capability, and the annual cost for the team size in section 2, with the arithmetic shown.

CITATIONS
Exactly 1 source list. Every inline marker resolves to an entry in it. The URL printed beside a claim must be the source its marker points to. No source you cannot give a URL for.

CONSISTENCY
Re-read your own numbers before you finish. A figure stated twice must agree. Quote the source's figure in the source's own units.

FIT
Before recommending any channel, programme or product, check that the asker in section 2 is eligible for it. Say plainly if they are not.

SELF-CHECK (required, at the end)
A section titled "Claims I could not fully verify" listing: every INFERRED or UNVERIFIABLE claim; every number whose source is older than 18 months; every contradiction; every claim resting on 1 non-primary source; every cost resting on an assumed team size; every marker that does not resolve; every page named in section 3 that you did not open, by URL.

OUTPUT
1. Summary: 5 to 8 findings, 1 sentence each, each tagged.
2. Main body, organised by the scope. Every number has a source and date.
3. Tables where asked. Every number tagged.
4. Open questions: 3 to 5.
5. Claims I could not fully verify.
6. The source list: title, URL, date accessed.

If you cannot trace a number to an acceptable source, mark it UNVERIFIABLE and move on. A short report I can trust is worth more than a long one I cannot.

PROCEED.
